*Fifty tips in.* _But the most useful thing to take away probably isn't in any of them: It's what writing them does to the *writer*._

I learn way more from writing a tip than from reading one. Writing these _forces the work_, paying attention to what is happening, reading, using the tools, trying things. You can't put "💡 Try this" in there until you've tried it. And the fact-check is important — an idea dies if I cannot corroborate it. For every tip you've read, *many* didn't make it: obsolete, too narrow, too broad, way too advanced, too basic, or it just didn't hold up.

AI is genuinely good at some of the time consuming parts: reading twenty sources, verifying links, checking whether I've already covered something, drafting a version I can react to. Real leverage, and I use it all the time.

But AI doesn't have very good *judgment* - at least not out of the box. Judgment is hard to relay. AI struggles to decide what's worth saying *here*, in this context – and to balance enthusiasm and skepticism. And there is *taste* (does this feel _good_?), and taste comes from experience.

_What I try to tell you isn't to start sharing tips like these_ (although that would be cool!), but to *consider to start writing more things down*: observations, reflections, things you learn, ideas. Perhaps you can call it a work journal or your field notes?

💡 *Try this: Set a goal to write a few sentences on what you experience each day for two weeks - say 100 words per day? You don't have to share anything, this is for you.*
